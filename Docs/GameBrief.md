# Ashfall: Black Meridian — Game Brief

## High concept

A solo-first, round-based first-person survival shooter set in a decommissioned coastal atmospheric research station after a failed storm-energy experiment. Each round intensifies the threat and physically changes the station: emergency shutters fail open, maintenance routes unlock, power reroutes, and the storm exposes new traversal lanes.

## Pillars

1. **Readable pressure:** clear enemy silhouettes, audio tells, and an unbroken combat loop.
2. **A map that tells the story:** the Black Meridian station evolves in authored phases rather than only scaling numbers.
3. **Tactile improvisation:** a compact arsenal with distinct handling plus temporary power-ups.
4. **Original identity:** industrial-coastal sci-fi horror, not a reproduction of another game's content.

## Vertical-slice rules

- Solo survival, rounds 1–12 are the initial playable target.
- Buy doors and weapons with salvage earned from kills and repairs.
- Each round begins after a short ready phase and ends when all active enemies are defeated.
- Every third round introduces a map phase change; round 6 is an elite stormbound wave.
- Defeat ends the run; the HUD shows round, salvage, health, ammo, and map phase.

## Initial content

- First-person controller with keyboard/mouse and Unity Input System gamepad bindings.
- Three weapons: Meridian sidearm, Breakwater shotgun, and Arc-9 rifle.
- Three enemy archetypes: shambler, sprinter, and storm brute.
- Power-ups: Overcharge (temporary damage), Salvage Surge (bonus currency), and Last Stand (temporary downed protection).
- One modular station map with a courtyard, lab wing, generator room, and storm-exposed rooftop route.
- Procedural Blender source assets for modular architecture, props, weapons, and enemy placeholder meshes.

## Art direction

Cinematic but performant: wet concrete, oxidized metal, hazard paint, teal storm light, amber emergency lighting, volumetric fog where supported, and strong value separation for gameplay readability. Assets are intentionally original and generated from the project's Blender scripts.
